# Development Log

How the project was built, step by step, including what went wrong. Every fix was checked by
comparing against Gazebo's ground truth (`gz model -p`), not by eye.

## Phase 0: Warehouse AMR (starting point)
A 1.5 × 1.0 m, 150 kg differential-drive AMR with front and rear LiDARs merged into one scan,
IMU, encoders and 4 ToF sensors, in a 12 × 8 m warehouse.

**Problem: the SLAM map was scattered and doubled.** Four causes, found one at a time:
1. **LiDAR merger offsets** (0.7 / −0.7 / π) were added *on top of* TF, so the front scan was
   drawn 0.7 m too far forward and the rear scan was flipped. → All offsets set to 0.
2. **Two publishers of `odom → base_link`** (diff drive controller and EKF). → Controller TF off.
3. **Casters floated 5 cm above the floor**, so the robot tipped back and dragged. → Sphere casters touching the floor.
4. **Cylinder wheels touched on their inner rim**, so the real track was 0.72 m instead of 0.80 m
   and the robot turned 11 % more than odometry said. → Sphere wheel collisions.
   After: odometry matched truth exactly (spin −1.5330 vs −1.5330 rad).

The EKF was then changed to fuse **wheel velocities + IMU yaw rate**.

Then Nav2 + AMCL were set up on the warehouse map.

## Step 1: Service robot model
3-tray café cart, 0.60 × 0.52 m, ~31 kg. Two rear BLDC wheels (r 7.5 cm, 0.47 m apart),
two front swivel wheels, LiDAR on a 1.25 m mast, RealSense-like depth camera at 0.88 m tilted
20° down, IMU, 1024-CPR encoders, 4 ToF sensors at 12 cm.

Checked: LiDAR distances within 1 cm of truth, depth within 2 cm, odometry = truth, EKF drift 0.2–0.3 %.

**Key finding: the mast LiDAR can't see anything lower than 1.25 m**, so tables, chairs and
boxes are invisible to it. The navigation design had to account for this.

## Step 2: Café world
Generated by `gen_cafe_world.py` so the layout is edited in one place: a 12 × 8 m room,
6 round tables with chairs, a pillar, plants, a partition with a 1.2 m doorway, and a kitchen
with a counter, fridge, shelf and charging dock.

## Step 3: Mapping
SLAM Toolbox with an 11-waypoint scripted route. Final pose error: 7 mm / 0.014 rad.
As expected, the tables aren't in the map, because the LiDAR is above them.

## Step 4: Navigation (the hardest part)
- **3D voxel costmap layers** with LiDAR + depth camera (+ ToF in the local costmap). A 2D
  layer let the high LiDAR "clear" cells the camera had marked, so the robot drove into a box.
- **No-go zones** (`gen_cafe_zones.py`) around tables and behind the counter.
- **Footprint around the rear axle**: the front corners swing out ~0.65 m when turning, so
  table stops are 1.05 m from the table centre. Padding is 5 cm (10 cm made normal stops count as collisions).
- **Collision monitor**: slow down to 40 % near obstacles, plus a footprint "approach" check.
  A hard "stop" zone froze the robot next to furniture.
- **ToF converter** now reports `max_range` when clear; `inf` made the monitor stop the robot.
- **Planner/controller choice**: NavFn + Regulated Pure Pursuit. Rejected: Smac Hybrid-A\*
  (looping table approaches) and MPPI (timid turns, dithering in the doorway).

Tests: tour 7/7 with no box, 7/7 with a box in the aisle, 3/3 after moving it.
Arrival 4–13 cm, AMCL 0.3–5 cm, closest approach 17 cm.

## Step 5: Delivery routine
`delivery.py`: counter → wait for "Done" (or timeout) → each table → charger. If a stop is
unreachable it clears the costmaps, retries once, then skips and reports. `--serve` mode
queues orders from `/delivery/order`.

Tested: normal trip, serve + confirm, bad table rejected, blocked table skipped.

## Step 6: Dashboard and fault demo
An 11-panel live dashboard (turn rate from wheels/IMU/EKF, commanded vs actual speed, heading
estimates, LiDAR, depth, colour image, ToF, EKF vs AMCL path, AMCL correction, wheel speeds).

`wheel_fault:=1.15` makes the wheel odometry under-report turns by 13 %. The wheel-only heading
drifted 20–35°, but EKF + AMCL agreed and every goal was still reached.

## Ops lessons
- `ros2 topic pub -w 1 --once` waits for a subscriber, so one-shot messages aren't lost.
- Stale Fast DDS files in `/dev/shm` can break discovery; `tools/stop_sim.sh` cleans them.
- In stop scripts, use the `[x]` trick in `pkill -f` patterns so the script doesn't kill itself.
